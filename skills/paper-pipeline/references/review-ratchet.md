# The review ratchet — how to fix a caught overclaim, and how to pay for it

Cited by `paper-adversarial-review`, `pc-panel-review` and `harden-paper`. It lived as three
near-identical paragraphs in those three skills until 2026-07-30; three copies of a rule drift, and
the drift is invisible because each copy reads fine on its own.

## The rule

**Review only ever ADDS text.** Every round deposits hedges, caveats, clarifications and one more
citation. Nothing in a review round removes anything — that is what makes it a ratchet, and why a
paper that survived four rounds reads like armor.

**When a review catches an overclaim, inline-hedging is the LAST resort.** In order:

1. **TIGHTEN** the claim to what is actually true. Usually shorter *and* correct — the strongest fix
   and the one that gets skipped, because it means admitting the smaller result is the result.
2. **CUT it.** A claim you cannot support is not costing you what you think it is.
3. **Move the caveat to Threats to Validity**, stated once. Adversarial reviewers read Threats first,
   and an honest limitation there reads as strength; the same sentence inline reads as flinching.
4. **Only then inline-hedge** — and if you find yourself here often, the contribution is mis-sized,
   not under-qualified.

## Paying the ratchet back

**After applying a round's consensus fixes, run `tighten-paper`** to strip the mass the round
deposited. Structure-pruning is the counterweight; without it the paper monotonically inflates.

Observed cost of skipping it: one session, seven overclaim fixes plus one panel round added roughly a
page of pure hedge density.

## What this rule does NOT license

- It is not permission to skip a gate. Trimming what a *passed* gate deposited is anti-churn; not
  running a gate at all is a hole.
- It is not a reason to cut a **Limitations** bullet. Those are the hedges that earned their place —
  they are stated once, in the right section, which is exactly what step 3 asks for.
