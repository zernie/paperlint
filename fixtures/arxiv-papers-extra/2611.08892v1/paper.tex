\begin{abstract}
Recursive self-training of language models, a setting studied in model collapse, can reduce
vocabulary while making frequent words, mostly function words, less dominant.
We fine-tune English GPT-2 on its own output for nine generations, vary the training-data
sampling temperature, and measure every model with one fixed sampler. As training temperature
rises, R\'enyi entropy changes turn from loss to gain at distinct temperatures: min-entropy
first, then collision entropy, Shannon entropy and observed richness. Between the outer
crossings, word types fall while the most-common word becomes less dominant. Untruncated
training at $T=1$ lies in this interval: at 13,000 words, generation~9 has 24\% fewer types than
generation~0 yet collision entropy is 0.91 bits higher. The ordering persists with a finer grid,
different measurement samplers, a fixed word budget and five fresh training seeds. Min-entropy again crosses
before richness in four further settings (in Hindi only at interpolated roots) and in 12 of
13 distinct measurements under eight text representations. Next-token identities and a
categorical simulation isolate decoding and sampling effects; the neural crossing order
remains empirical. Fine-tuning on human text can produce the same split while likelihood
improves, and no single order's sign reliably identifies worse likelihood across the tested
settings. We recommend reporting entropy profiles at stated budgets with an independent
quality measure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Training language models on synthetic text can erode quality and diversity
\citep{shumailov2024,dohmatob2024}. Single diversity scores measure different properties:
richness counts observed word types, whereas higher-order entropies increasingly emphasize
common words. Losing rare words while spreading usage more evenly among those retained can
therefore lower richness and raise higher-order entropy. The reported direction depends on
which property is measured.

We fine-tune English GPT-2 on its own samples for nine generations, varying the temperature
and nucleus (top-$p$) threshold of the \emph{training sampler}. Every model is measured with
one fixed sampler ($T=1$, top-$p=0.95$). As training temperature rises, min-entropy $\Hinf$
turns from loss to gain first, near $T=0.927$, followed by collision entropy $\Htwo$
($0.962$), Shannon entropy $H_1$ ($0.997$) and observed richness $\Hz$ ($1.035$)
(Figure~\ref{fig:crossings}). Between the outer crossings, word types fall while the most-common
word becomes less dominant. Untruncated sampling at $T=1$ lies inside this interval.

The outer ordering survives 12 of 13 distinct measurements under eight representations and
recurs under LoRA, in OLMo and in German and Hindi GPT-2 (Hindi only by interpolation).
We test the diagnostic value of these changes: truncation lowers entropy relative to untruncated
training in ten settings, but no order's sign reliably identifies worse likelihood, and human
adaptation can produce the same split while likelihood improves.

Diversity profiles are standard \citep{hill1973,tothmeresz1995}.
\citet{drayson2025detection} already observed rising $n$-gram diversity with worsening perplexity
in recursive GPT-2 under pure sampling ($T=1$, no truncation). Our contribution is the measured
ordering of sign changes across entropy orders, its sensitivity to measurement choices, and
tests of its diagnostic limits. Five fresh training seeds replicate the primary ordering
(Appendix~\ref{app:freshseeds}). Section~\ref{sec:crossings} presents the main crossing
result; the later experiments test its transfer and diagnostic limits, with detailed controls
in the appendices.

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Diversity profiles.} R\'enyi entropy \citep{renyi1961}, extended by limits at orders zero
and one, includes observed log-richness, Shannon entropy \citep{shannon1948} and collision entropy. Yule's $K$ has been studied as a constancy measure of text \citep{tweedie1998}. Collision
entropy is closely related and has also been studied in this role \citep{tanakaishii2015}. In ecology, $\sum_i p_i^2$ is
Simpson's concentration \citep{simpson1949} and $2^{H_\alpha}$ are Hill numbers
\citep{hill1973,jost2006}; reporting a profile over orders, whose members weight rare and abundant species differently, is
long-standing practice \citep{tothmeresz1995}. The Vendi Score and its order-indexed
extensions apply the same idea to the spectra of similarity matrices
\citep{friedman2023vendi,pasarkar2024cousins}, coverage-rarefied Hill--Shannon diversity has been
used to compare the claims made by language models with web search \citep{wright2026epistemic},
and higher-order R\'enyi entropies of repeated sequences separate model text from natural language
\citep{tanakaishii2026repeated}. \citet{zouhar2023}
sweep R\'enyi orders to evaluate tokenizers, and \citet{france2026} find that entropy- and
concentration-based measures can rank human and model text differently. Recommending a profile
is therefore not new; our question is how the \emph{sign} of each order's change behaves under
controlled recursion.

\paragraph{Generation and recursive training.} Distinct-$n$ \citep{li2016} and self-BLEU
\citep{zhu2018} measure output diversity, MAUVE \citep{pillutla2021} measures distributional
fit to reference text, and diversity metrics differ in whether they capture form or content
\citep{tevet2021diversity}. Recursive degradation has been studied empirically and through
scaling laws \citep{shumailov2024,dohmatob2024}, also in image models \citep{alemohammad2023},
including effects on
lexical and syntactic diversity \citep{guo2024} and repetition \citep{herel2024collapse}; even
without recursion, synthetic training data over-concentrate $n$-gram features \citep{zhu2025synthesize}. Curation, the share of fresh data, and
whether synthetic data replace or accumulate with real data all change the outcome
\citep{ferbach2024curated,briesch2023,gerstgrasser2024collapse,kazdan2025collapse}.
Next-token concentration has been used to track collapse, with perplexity-based filtering as a
remedy \citep{gambetta2026surprise}, and the time a model takes to forget a symbol has been
analyzed theoretically \citep{suresh2025forgetting}. Definitions of collapse vary
\citep{schaeffer2025position}. We define degradation by a stated reference-likelihood cutoff and
evaluate diversity changes separately.

\paragraph{Closest prior results.} \citet{seddik2024howbad} derive the collision ratchet, with
its rate, for recursive maximum-likelihood training of categorical models;
Proposition~\ref{prop:ratchet} (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}) extends the argument to upper-set
truncation. \citet{dohmatob2024} model truncation as cutting the tail of the distribution and
low temperature as narrowing it, and \citet{marchi2024heat} prove that closed-loop retraining at a
non-trivial sampling temperature degenerates, either to a few outputs or to a uniform ``heat
death'', unless enough fresh data enter each round. Under untruncated, infinite-sample recursive tempering of a
non-uniform categorical law, all positive orders switch sign at $T=1$ and support size stays
fixed (Propositions~\ref{prop:temper} and~\ref{prop:sens}). The separate neural roots depart
from this baseline, without isolating the effects of sampling, autoregressive contexts,
refitting or the fixed truncated reader.
\citet{drayson2025detection} vary the decoder
in recursive GPT-2: under pure sampling ($T=1$, no truncation), $n$-gram diversity rises while
perplexity worsens; all five other decoders lose $n$-gram diversity. Meanwhile,
\citet{kovac2025} link lexical diversity to recursive distribution shift. We measure the order
of zero crossings across the R\'enyi spectrum, test its
sensitivity to budget and representation, and ask what it lets a diagnostic infer.

\paragraph{Recent and concurrent work.} \citet{hu2026profiles} independently recommend
diversity profiles across orders and scales. \citet{skobelev2026finetuning} measure diversity by collision
probability, bound the absolute model--target collision gap by the square root of the KL
divergence from target to model, and show that supervised fine-tuning can leave a model
under- or over-dispersed. Their setting is fine-tuning toward a fixed target at order~2;
ours tracks the sign changes across R\'enyi orders during recursive self-training. Other studies address diversity loss in
post-training \citep{karouzos2026where}, entropy-based early warnings \citep{zhang2026silent},
recursion in larger models \citep{nakamura2026demystifying}, the loss of rare forms within
languages \citep{vanmassenhove2026tail}, ecological diversity measures for linguistic structure
\citep{gude2026aligned}, and the diversity of synthetic data sources
\citep{schaffelder2026eggs}. Concurrently, \citet{liu2026fragility} trace rapid lexical collapse
in vLLM to draws shared across requests and replayed across generations. Changing seeds removes
the rapid loss in their controls, revising their earlier account. Our Transformers harness
keys streams by replicate, generation and phase, sharing them across paired arms but not
replaying an unchanged seed across generations. \citet{liu2026restructures} finds that self-training amplifies
surface markers while deeper syntactic structures decay, and that 100-word-window type--token
ratio rises. In our own English GPT-2 runs at $T=1$, the seed-mean ratio over non-overlapping
100-word windows at common length rises from $0.746$ to $0.841$ while the common-length type inventory falls by
43\%.

\section{The measure}
\label{sec:measure}

\paragraph{Setup.} Let $p=(p_i)_{i\in\V}$ be a distribution over a vocabulary, either a
next-token softmax or the empirical word frequencies $\ph_v=f_v/N$ of a text. For finite
$\alpha\ge0$ with $\alpha\ne1$, the R\'enyi entropy is
$H_\alpha(p)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log_2\sum_{i:p_i>0}p_i^\alpha$. Thus
$H_0=\log_2|\operatorname{supp}p|$, and the limiting orders are Shannon entropy
$H_1=-\sum_i p_i\log_2p_i$ and min-entropy $H_\infty=-\log_2\max_i p_i$. Entropy does not
increase with the order, so $H_0\ge H_1\ge\Htwo\ge H_\infty$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).
Collision entropy is $\Htwo=-\log_2 C$, where $C(p)=\sum_i p_i^2$ is the probability that two
independent draws coincide.

\paragraph{Yule's $K$.} In empirical-probability form \citep{yule1944},
\begin{equation}
K = 10^4\,\frac{\sum_v f_v^2 - N}{N^2}
 = 10^4\Big(2^{-\Htwo(\ph)}-\tfrac1N\Big).
\label{eq:yulek}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:yulek}, at fixed $N$ Yule's $K$ is a strictly decreasing function of $\Htwo$, so
for texts of equal length a fall in $K$ and a rise in $\Htwo$ are the same event. The $1/N$
offset can break this correspondence between texts of different lengths, which is one reason we
compare texts at matched budgets. The offset $10^4/N$ vanishes as $N$ grows, so for long texts
$K\approx10^4\cdot2^{-\Htwo(\ph)}$.

\paragraph{Measurement.} For a token string $x_{1:N}$ we compute $\Htwo(x_{1:N})=\Htwo(\ph)$
from its observed counts, so no model probabilities or logits are needed. Higher orders
emphasize common types, whereas $\Hz$ only counts observed types. We use ``evenness'' loosely
for this sensitivity to concentration; $\Htwo$ also depends on richness and is not normalized
by the size of the support.

Order~2 has Simpson's unbiased pair-count estimator \citep{simpson1949}. Under i.i.d.\ sampling,
$\widehat C_{\mathrm u}=\sum_v f_v(f_v-1)/[N(N-1)]$ is unbiased for the collision probability,
and $K=10^4(N-1)\widehat C_{\mathrm u}/N$. This does not make $-\log_2\widehat C_{\mathrm u}$
an unbiased estimate of entropy. Integer orders above two have analogous falling-factorial
estimators, fixed-sample counterparts of those analyzed under Poisson sampling by
\citet{acharya2017renyi}
(Appendix~\ref{app:factorial-estimator}).

\section{Effects of decoding on next-token entropy}
\label{sec:pertoken}

For standard temperature and nucleus controls \citep{holtzman2020}, the following facts
are known. Majorization orders tempered distributions \citep{marshall2011inequalities},
implying temperature monotonicity of positive-order R\'enyi entropies
\citep{mattei2026welltempered}. \citet{nadeem2020systematic} give the Shannon entropy-reduction
counterparts for top-$k$, nucleus and low-temperature sampling;
\citet[App.~J]{skobelev2026finetuning} concurrently state order-2 temperature monotonicity
and a support-size cap. We restate the order-2 facts, with proofs in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs},
for the document-level analysis in \S\ref{sec:vbridge}.

\begin{proposition}[Temperature monotonicity]
\label{thm:temp}
For a softmax $p_i(T)\propto e^{z_i/T}$ over finite, non-constant logits, $\Htwo(p_T)$ is strictly increasing in $T$ on $(0,\infty)$, with $\Htwo\to\log_2 m$ as $T\to 0^+$, where $m$ is the number of tied maxima (so the limit is $0$ if the maximum is unique), and $\Htwo\to\log_2|\V|$ as $T\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}
With $\beta=1/T$, $I\sim p_T$ and $\mu=\E[z_I]$,
$\frac{d}{d\beta}\sum_i p_i^2 = 2\sum_i p_i^2(z_i-\mu)=2\Cov(p_I,z_I)>0$, because $p_i$
increases with $z_i$. Raising the temperature therefore lowers the next-token collision
probability.

\begin{proposition}[Truncation strictly lowers $\Htwo$]
\label{thm:trunc}
Let $S$ be an upper set, meaning $\min_{i\in S}p_i\ge\max_{j\notin S}p_j$ (top-$k$ and top-$p$ selection both produce one), with retained mass $a=\sum_{i\in S}p_i\in(0,1)$, and let $q_i=p_i/a$ on $S$. Then $\Htwo(q)<\Htwo(p)$.
\end{proposition}
Conditioning on an upper set gives $\E[p_I\mid I\in S]\ge\E[p_I]$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:upperset}),
and renormalizing then yields $C(q)=a^{-1}\E[p_I\mid I\in S]>C(p)$. Keeping the top 20 of 100
equally likely tokens already costs $\log_2 5\approx2.3$ bits.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-support cap]
\label{prop:cap}
If sampling is restricted to a set $S$ with $|S|=k$, then for any reshaping of the surviving mass (for example, any temperature applied within $S$), $\Htwo\le\log_2 k$, with equality if and only if the distribution is uniform on $S$.
\end{proposition}
The cap applies to a \emph{fixed} retained set. Raising the temperature before top-$p$
selection can enlarge that set, and the retained set differs from context to context, so the
cap by itself does not bound document-level entropy independently of temperature.

\section{From softmax to document: the \texorpdfstring{$V$}{V}-bridge}
\label{sec:vbridge}

Let $p_{c_t}$ be the conditional token distributions at $N$ contexts and
$\pbar=N^{-1}\sum_t p_{c_t}$ their mean. We first relate the collision entropy of this mean to
the variation across contexts, and then connect it to sampled frequencies.

\begin{lemma}[Variance decomposition]
\label{lem:vbridge}
With mean per-token collision $\Cbar=\frac1N\sum_t\sum_v p_{c_t}(v)^2$ and across-context dispersion $V=\sum_v\Var_t[p_{c_t}(v)]\ge0$, where $\Var_t$ averages over the $N$ contexts,
\begin{equation}
C(\pbar)=\Cbar - V,\qquad \Htwo(\pbar)=-\log_2(\Cbar-V).
\label{eq:vbridge}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

This is the additive Gini--Simpson partition \citep{rao1982,lande1996} applied to conditional
token distributions: it separates within-context concentration from between-context variation. Deterministic conditionals have zero per-token entropy, yet their mean
can spread its mass over many different argmaxes. It also explains why the next-token results
of \S\ref{sec:pertoken} do not fix the direction of document-level change: a change of
temperature changes both $\Cbar$ and $V$, and $\Htwo(\pbar)$ rises exactly when
$\Cbar-V$ falls.

For a token process of fixed length $N$, let $p_{c_t}$ be the \emph{sampling law} given the
history and $\ph_N$ the empirical frequencies. Each one-hot sampled token minus its sampling law is
a martingale difference, so $\E\|\ph_N-\pbar_N\|_2^2\le 1/N$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). The bound compares empirical
frequencies with the average sampling law along the same random path; fixed-population
convergence and autoregressive entropy bias require separate arguments.

\paragraph{Fixed-context measurement.} On softmax probabilities of Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct at 200
fixed 48-token contexts from four Gutenberg books (Appendix~\ref{app:figvals}), $T=0.1$ gives
$\Cbar=0.90$ and $V=0.88$, so $\Htwo(\pbar)=5.65$ bits, far above the value $-\log_2\Cbar=0.14$
bits implied by the mean per-context collision; computing $V$ directly reproduces \eqref{eq:vbridge} to within $1.1\times10^{-8}$ at every temperature.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

Word-level R\'enyi values apply NLTK \texttt{word\_tokenize} \citep{bird2009nltk}, version 3.9.1,
to the original text, lowercase the resulting tokens, and drop tokens that contain no letter or
number; punctuation attached to a
word stays with it. The same English-trained tokenizer is applied to German, Spanish and Hindi.
Chinese uses Han characters
(U+4E00--U+9FFF). Next-token softmax values use each model's byte-pair encoding (BPE) vocabulary. We
compare changes only within one representation.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Each generation samples a training corpus from the current model and
fine-tunes it on that corpus. We vary training temperature $T$ and top-$p$; measurements use
one fixed \emph{measurement sampler} ($T=1$, top-$p=0.95$, top-$k$ disabled; the ``probe''
in the code), holding the reader constant across trained models.
We call top-$p=0.80$ at $T=1$ the \emph{truncated} arm and top-$p=1$ at $T=1$ the
\emph{untruncated} arm. Random numbers are shared across arms: each draw is keyed by seed,
generation and phase, and all arms use the same pool of real-text prompts. The LoRA \citep{hu2022lora}
arms of a replicate start from one saved initial adapter, verified by a SHA-256
fingerprint of its trainable tensors. PEFT's zero-initialized $B$ matrices
\citep{mangrulkar2022peft} yield identical initial outputs even with different random $A$ matrices.

\paragraph{Budgets and seeds.} Richness depends strongly on the length of a text, so every
comparison states its budget. A \emph{common-length} comparison truncates the generation-0 and
generation-9 texts of each seed to their shared length, and a \emph{fixed-budget} comparison
uses the first 13,000 words of every text. Most studies use three seeds; the primary English grid and its refinements, the OLMo and Hindi grids, the
Pythia study and the checkpoint-branch intervention use five (Pythia's first stage was later extended to
eight, and the independent-rerun comparison in Appendix~\ref{app:detect} used ten). Held-out cross-perplexity is the exponential of mean next-token negative log
likelihood on real text disjoint from training.

\paragraph{Boundaries and sampling defaults.} Before training samples are concatenated, the
harness removes the padding-token ID. For GPT-2 this also removes the end-of-sequence (EOS)
token. OLMo-2 retains its distinct EOS, and Qwen2.5-Instruct retains its primary EOS but removes
its second stop ID, which equals padding. The German and Hindi checkpoints declare an ordinary subword as their EOS,
so their continuations end only when that subword happens to be sampled
(Appendix~\ref{app:repr}). Every sampling call in the training harness passes the temperature,
top-$p$ and top-$k=0$ explicitly and otherwise inherits the checkpoint's generation configuration. For
Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct that configuration sets a repetition penalty of 1.1, which therefore
applied to every Qwen-Instruct training corpus and measurement. No other sampled checkpoint sets one;
\S\ref{sec:richness} tests its effect with a matched rerun.

\paragraph{Models and data.} The primary model under full fine-tuning (full-FT) is GPT-2 with 124M parameters (listed as 117M
in \citealt{radford2019}). Robustness settings use German \citep{dbmdz2020germangpt2} and Spanish
\citep{flaxcommunity2021gpt2spanish} GPT-2 checkpoints, gpt2-hindi \citep{surajp2021gpt2hindi},
Wenzhong-GPT2-110M \citep{zhang2022fengshenbang}, Qwen2.5-0.5B base and Instruct
\citep{qwen2025}, and SmolLM2-360M base and Instruct \citep{allal2025smollm2}.
Instruction-tuned checkpoints receive the same raw literary prefixes, without chat templates.
OLMo-2-1B \citep{olmo2}, whose released checkpoint OLMo-2-0425-1B has 1.48B parameters, extends the temperature grid in scale, and
Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct supplies next-token distributions on fixed contexts. English training text
is five books from NLTK's Gutenberg selection (Appendix~\ref{app:figvals}), with Austen's
\emph{Persuasion} held out, and
Appendix~\ref{app:multiwork} evaluates three further held-out works. German, Spanish and
Chinese use Project Gutenberg literature and Hindi uses Wikisource. The genres are not
uniform: the Spanish training text includes travel essays and the Chinese held-out text is a
travelogue (Appendix~\ref{app:figvals}).

\subsection{Ordered zero crossings across training temperatures}
\label{sec:crossings}

\paragraph{Design.} We sweep the training sampler's temperature and top-$p$ for English GPT-2
under full fine-tuning. A coarse grid of 15 cells (Table~\ref{tab:crossings}) is refined near
$T=1$ with five further temperatures at top-$p=1$ ($0.85$, $0.95$, $0.98$, $1.02$ and $1.05$).
Every cell has five seeds and nine generations, 900 trained generation-steps in all; corpus
sizes and optimizer settings are in Appendix~\ref{app:figvals}. The count analyses use a separate
eight-batch dump from the measurement sampler, while the logged $\Htwo$ series uses two batches.
Continuations are concatenated without a separator before counting; inserting separators changes
no conclusion (Appendix~\ref{app:repr}). A grid with generation batch 32 serves as a protocol
sensitivity check (Appendix~\ref{app:dense}).

For each order $\alpha$ we define $T^\ast(H_\alpha)$ as the first temperature at which the
seed-mean change from generation~0 to generation~9 crosses zero from below, using linear
interpolation between sampled cells. Crossings need not be unique: at top-$p=0.80$, richness
turns upward near $T=1.240$ and back down near $1.340$.

\paragraph{Refined crossings.} On the top-$p=1$ axis every order falls at $T=0.70$ and rises at
$T=1.25$, but each turns at a different temperature: $T^\ast(\Hinf)=0.927$,
$T^\ast(\Htwo)=0.962$, $T^\ast(H_1)=0.997$ and $T^\ast(\Hz)=1.035$. At four measured
temperatures ($0.95$, $0.98$, $1.00$ and $1.02$) richness falls while min-entropy rises; the
interval between the outer crossings is interpolated from these cells. We call
$T^\ast(\Hinf)<T^\ast(\Hz)$ the \emph{outer ordering} and the complete sequence
$T^\ast(\Hinf)<T^\ast(\Htwo)<T^\ast(H_1)<T^\ast(\Hz)$ the \emph{full ordering}. Resampling the
five seeds jointly across all cells (4,000 bootstrap draws) gives 95\% intervals of
$[0.917,0.950]$, $[0.959,0.963]$, $[0.993,1.001]$ and $[1.030,1.039]$, and every draw keeps the
full ordering. These intervals hold the grid fixed and so leave out interpolation error, which
is not negligible: refining the grid moved the $\Htwo$ root from $0.949$ to $0.961$ and then to
$0.962$, and the first move fell outside the coarse-grid interval of $[0.941,0.956]$. The third
decimal therefore describes the interpolant rather than the resolution of the experiment. On the coarse
grid, individual seeds follow the same ordering wherever all four roots are bracketed: in 5 of
5, 4 of 4 and 5 of 5 seeds at top-$p=1.0$, $0.8$ and $0.6$. The two top-$p=0.4$ cells bracket
no crossing. Across 18 sampled orders, all 4,000 common-length resamples preserve decreasing
crossing temperature with increasing order. Without interpolation, every measured temperature
has at most one change from negative to positive along those orders, in each seed and in the
mean (Appendix~\ref{app:posthocanalyses}). This single-crossing statement concerns only the
sampled orders; where signs differ, neither generation uniformly dominates the other on the
R\'enyi/Hill profile \citep{patil1982diversity,tothmeresz1995}. These analyses were not prespecified.

\paragraph{A fixed budget.} With common-length budgets, training at $T=1$ gives
$\Delta\Hz=-0.81$ bits (43\% fewer types) and $\Delta\Htwo=+0.94$ bits. Every grid corpus has
more than 13,000 words, so the analysis can be repeated at that fixed budget in every cell. At
$T=1$ it gives $\Delta\Hz=-0.400$ bits (24\% fewer types) and $\Delta\Htwo=+0.907$ bits, and
the roots become $0.936$, $0.963$, $0.990$ and $1.022$. The same four cells show falling
richness and rising min-entropy, and all 4,000 resamples keep the ordering. The budget changes
the size of the richness loss and the width of the interval. At tested prefixes of at most
1,000 words, however, generation~9 has more types at $T=1$ in every seed; the split appears
in four of five seeds at 5,000 words and all five at tested budgets from 8,000 upward.

\paragraph{Word frequencies behind the split.} At common length and $T=1$, \emph{the} falls from
4.9\% to 1.8\% of words. The ten commonest generation-0 types, all function words, account
for 104\% of the collision-probability decrease; the rest offset it slightly. Removing the
50 commonest types makes all four mean entropy changes negative
(Appendix~\ref{app:posthocanalyses}). This rise describes reduced function-word concentration
and could accompany grammatical degradation, which unigram counts do not test; reference
likelihood worsens here.

\paragraph{Apparent recovery while quality worsens.} At top-$p=0.60$, raising the training
temperature from $1.00$ to $1.18$ moves $\Delta\Htwo$ toward zero, from $-1.49$ to $-1.26$
bits, while cross-perplexity worsens from $11.6$ to $37.3$ times its generation-0 value; both
changes occur in all five seeds. Richness stays near $-4.4$ bits. A monitor watching $\Htwo$
alone would read this as partial recovery. At top-$p=0.80$, raising the temperature from $1.00$ to $1.09$ also moves $\Delta\Htwo$ toward zero, but there
cross-perplexity improves in four of five seeds.

\paragraph{Scope of the theory.} Tempering changes common and rare types at
different rates (Propositions~\ref{prop:temper} and~\ref{prop:sens}), and i.i.d.\ resampling
biases collision probability and observed support in known ways (Proposition~\ref{prop:rarefy}).
Higher-order plug-in entropy bias can have either sign. Predicting the crossing order through
nine neural refits under a fixed reader requires more than these one-step population and
sampling results. A
categorical simulation alternating tempering, 13,000 draws and refitting with uniform
smoothing of weight $0.02$, starting from our
measured unigram law, reproduces the outer ordering; the $\Hinf$ and $\Htwo$ roots remain in
the same grid bracket, and the neural roots remain empirical (Appendix~\ref{app:crossings}).

\subsection{Truncation lowers collision entropy relative to untruncated training}
\label{sec:robust}

top-$p=0.95$ with 10\% real text mixed into each training corpus. It starts from a GPT-2
checkpoint adapted to real text (Appendix~\ref{app:figvals}), whereas the temperature grid
starts from pretrained GPT-2. With top-$p=0.80$, word-level $\Htwo$ falls from $6.85$ to

under the same two-epoch schedule. On the truncated arms the models also grow more confident on
human text while their likelihood of that text falls (Appendix~\ref{sec:confquality}). A rerun also shows disagreement among standard diversity metrics (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}).

\subsection{An exception traced to a sampler setting}
\label{sec:richness}

In Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct under LoRA, the truncated arm loses 59\% of its types ($\Hz$ falls by
$1.30$ bits) while $\Htwo$ rises by $0.47$ bits and $\Hinf$ by $1.62$, and cross-perplexity is
22 times worse (Tables~\ref{tab:dissociate} and~\ref{tab:qwenrp}). The text has fewer types but a less concentrated
frequency distribution, so rising collision entropy coexists here with both lost richness and
worse reference likelihood. SmolLM2-360M-Instruct, from a different family, contracts normally at
every order. On the Qwen checkpoint, sampled with the repetition penalty discussed below, the
effect holds across truncation levels: at the fixed 13,000-word budget $\Htwo$ rises in every seed at every tested top-$p$ from $0.60$ to $1.00$,
though only by $0.07$ bits at $0.60$. Leakage of other scripts does not explain it:
non-ASCII words, mostly Chinese and Cyrillic, make up $0.6\%$ of the first 13,000 words at
generation~0 and $0.5\%$ on the truncated arm at generation~9.

Removing the inherited repetition penalty eliminates this exception. The checkpoint sets it to
1.1, inherited in every phase (\S\ref{sec:setup}), down-weighting tokens already in the context.
We reran both arms and the checkpoint's three other training samplers at penalty 1.0, matching
initial adapters, random streams, corpus and budgets (Table~\ref{tab:qwenrp}). Without the penalty, generation-0 collision entropy is $6.57$
bits, close to the base model's, and the truncated arm contracts like every other setting: at
13,000 words it loses 86\% of its types, $\Hz$, $H_1$ and $\Htwo$ fall by $2.84$, $2.67$ and
$1.18$ bits ($\Htwo$ in all three seeds), and $\Hinf$ falls by $0.19$ on average. The untruncated
arm shows the weaker split of the other untruncated arms (\S\ref{sec:sweep}), losing 26\% of its types while $\Htwo$ holds ($+0.02$) and $\Hinf$ rises ($+0.43$). The
top-$p=0.60$ and $0.95$ arms now contract as well, with $\Htwo$ falling by $1.53$ and $0.79$ bits
in every seed, while training at $T=1.5$ still raises it. The other tables and detection pool retain the labeled penalized runs: they show that
another sampler setting can produce the same richness--concentration disagreement.

\subsection{What the profile can and cannot diagnose}
\label{sec:detect}

We test profile movement, discrimination and threshold transfer. A run is labeled degraded
when held-out cross-perplexity reaches twice its generation-0 value; this measures fit to one
reference text. Appendix~\ref{app:detect} gives protocols, controls and an intervention study.

\paragraph{Movement and sign.} Let $M_g=\max(|\Delta\Hz|,|\Delta\Hinf|)$ at generation $g$,
and let $D_g$ indicate that the two changes have opposite signs and both exceed $0.20$ bits in
magnitude, a floor chosen to reduce sensitivity to sampling noise. The development pool has 95
trajectories above the cutoff, the 75 coarse-grid runs and 20 Pythia-160m runs from the first
stage of the frozen-detector study (Appendix~\ref{app:detect}), together with six no-training
controls and three runs adapted on human text. The trajectory score $\max_g M_g$ reaches AUROC $0.994$, but six of its nine
negatives never trained, so this mainly shows that it detects movement. The three alarm rules behave differently (Table~\ref{tab:alarms}, Appendix~\ref{app:detect}):
magnitude alone fires on 92 of 95 above-cutoff runs, and adding the sign clause catches all 95.
Without its floor the clause also fires on every unchanged model, and even with the floor it flags all
three human-data adaptations, whose profiles change by $\Delta\Hz=-0.636$ and
$\Delta\Hinf=+0.433$ bits at 13,000 words while cross-perplexity improves to $0.60$ times its
starting value. These controls use top-$k=50$; the alarm also flags all five human adaptations
under the primary protocol (Appendix~\ref{app:freshcontrols}).



\paragraph{Cross-setting classification.} On 660 corpora from 16 settings, we choose a feature
pair by nested leave-one-setting-out validation and score it on the held-out setting. The mean
AUROC over the 14 folds that contain both classes is $0.897$. Selection picks
$(\Htwo,\text{distinct-}2)$ in 11 folds, $(\Hz,\Htwo)$ in two and $(\Hz,H_1)$ in one. Evaluated
as a fixed pair in every fold, $(\Htwo,\text{distinct-}2)$ has the highest mean of the 15 pairs,
$0.963$, against $0.961$ for $(\Hz,\Htwo)$ and $0.917$ for $(\Hz,H_1)$; differences this small
do not single out a best pair. A one-feature post-hoc baseline exceeds both leading fixed pairs: unsigned richness change $|\Delta\Hz|$
scores $0.970$, while generation index alone, which uses no text, scores $0.908$.
The nested and fixed-pair numbers evaluate different procedures,
so their gap is not an estimate of selection bias. The worst fold is English $T=2$, where nested
selection chooses $(\Hz,H_1)$ and scores $0.194$. Appendices~\ref{app:posthocanalyses} and~\ref{app:familyholdout} give the in-sample fit and family holdouts. Four settings are
Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct runs sampled with the checkpoint's repetition penalty.

\paragraph{Pythia: one training regime, three decoders.} Pythia-160m \citep{biderman2023pythia}
is trained with five seeds at each of three decoders under a single regime: pure recursion,
learning rate $10^{-5}$ and one epoch. The $2\times$ cutoff on \emph{Persuasion} splits the
untruncated $T=1$ arm, with three seeds below it ($1.84$, $1.93$ and $1.94$) and two above
($2.07$ and $2.09$), and all ten runs at top-$p=0.8$ or $T=2$ (top-$p=0.95$) exceed it. The
exploratory endpoint score
$\max(|\Delta\Hz|,|\Delta\Htwo|)$ has AUROC $0.944$, the same as richness alone. That figure
rests on 30 of 30 concordant comparisons across decoders but only 4 of 6 within the $T=1$ arm
(AUROC $0.667$), so the pooled ranking mostly separates decoders rather than quality within a
decoder. One-sided scores stay near chance ($0.472$--$0.528$). Appendix~\ref{app:detect}
describes a preliminary frozen-detector test whose healthy runs used a different training recipe.

\paragraph{Changing the reference work.} With models, scores and cutoff held fixed, three more
books confirm that the GPT-2 grid loses likelihood: all 100 endpoints exceed the $2\times$
cutoff on every work, and at $T=1$ the mean ratios are $3.43$--$4.48$, against $3.83$ on
\emph{Persuasion}. Pythia's labels move more. \emph{Sense and Sensibility} places 11 of 15 runs
above the cutoff, while \emph{Moby Dick}, \emph{The Adventures of Buster Bear} and the three-book
aggregate place all 15 above it, which leaves no negative class and makes AUROC undefined
(Table~\ref{tab:multiwork}). Even within English literature, the choice of reference text changes the labels.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On the English GPT-2 training-temperature grid, min-entropy changes sign first and richness
last. Between these crossings, models have fewer word types and a less dominant most-common
word. At sufficient budgets, $T=1$ lies inside this interval under all three readers. The full
ordering holds at interpolated roots in every bracketed setting (Hindi has no measured split
cell), but in only 9 of 13 distinct measurements under eight representations; 12 keep the outer
ordering. The interval moves with the setting, and phrase-level spectra can reverse the order.

Report richness and higher orders at a stated budget and representation, with independent
quality measures. The same split occurs during human-text fine-tuning while likelihood improves,
so the signs of entropy changes do not identify degradation or its cause. Validate thresholds on healthy trajectories
under the intended decoder and reference text.

\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Theory and resolution.} The theory characterizes decoding and sampling effects;
the neural crossing order remains empirical. Most comparisons use three to five seeds;
coarse grids and linear interpolation limit precision, and bootstrap intervals exclude interpolation error. The Hindi
min-entropy root is fragile, and the vocabulary-composition comparison of
Appendix~\ref{sec:vocabdrift} is a heuristic based on three settings.

\paragraph{Quality and transfer.} Quality is measured only through reference likelihood and
automatic metrics, without human judgments. The other languages each use a single held-out work,
and we do not know which works the models saw in pretraining. Setting-level folds share model
families, Pythia's negative class depends on the reference, and the intervention results rest on
five seeds, so neither threshold transfer nor intervention timing is validated.

\paragraph{Protocol and scale.} The recursive models have at most $1.48$B parameters and train on
literary corpora of uneven genre. Because most settings train for two epochs per generation, the
number of updates changes with corpus length and with added real data. EOS handling differs by tokenizer,
and the GPT-2 EOS variant also halves the generation batch (Appendix~\ref{app:repr}). The
Qwen-Instruct runs in the main tables inherited a repetition penalty, which only the reruns of
\S\ref{sec:richness} remove. Training on Apple MPS is not bitwise deterministic.

\paragraph{Measurement and data.} Word, character and model-token entropies are not comparable,
a Han-only unit can lose almost all coverage, and unigram measures miss phrase-level loops. The
released count multisets recompute R\'enyi and $n$-gram metrics but not the 12,000-word detection
features (released as rows), self-BLEU, MAUVE or perplexity. Released endpoint, reader and control texts support reanalysis. The counts cover every
setting except two whose text was not kept (the four-arm study and the temperature sweep of
Appendix~\ref{sec:tempfails}). Several secondary runs lack exact revision or resume provenance (Appendix~\ref{app:figvals});
the endpoint MAUVE featurizer is also not fully recorded (Appendix~\ref{app:detect}).

\section*{Broader Impact Statement}
Treating a fall in diversity as evidence of poor quality can penalize legitimate specialization
or underrepresented varieties, so these diagnostics should be used with explicit units, coverage
checks and an independent quality evaluation before they filter data or models.

\subsection{Predictive confidence and reference likelihood}
\label{sec:confquality}

We measure predictive perplexity as $2^{\overline{H_1(q_t)}}$, the exponentiated mean entropy of
the truncated next-token law $q_t$ over held-out human contexts. It uses no generated samples and
differs both from model-scored sample perplexity and from word-level unigram entropy.
Cross-perplexity instead scores the observed reference tokens under the untruncated model. On the
top-$p=0.80$ arm, predictive perplexity under the measurement sampler falls from $26.3$ to $2.4$
while cross-perplexity rises from $50.3$ to $2{,}189$: more concentrated predictions accompany
worse reference likelihood (Figure~\ref{fig:selfcross}).

\subsection{Decoder and training temperature sweeps}
\label{sec:tempfails}

Propositions~\ref{thm:temp} and~\ref{thm:trunc} give opposing next-token effects, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:cap} bounds entropy only while the retained set stays fixed, so it gives no
document-level bound when contexts have different nuclei. Holding the training top-$p$ at $0.95$
and sweeping the \emph{training} temperature on GPT-2, generation-9 $\Htwo$ covers the whole
range: $6.5$ bits at $T=1$, $7.5$ at $T=1.0625$, $9.2$ at $T=1.125$ and $13.2$--$13.4$
for $T\ge1.5$. Tightening truncation instead moves it the other way, to $5.6$. The two knobs thus
push $\Htwo$ in opposite directions from a shared start. Quality is worse than at generation~0 ($50.3$) everywhere on the sweep, though not monotonically:
cross-perplexity is $437$, $306$, $381$, $999$, $2{,}860$, $8{,}072$, $20{,}923$ and $29{,}844$
at $T=1$, $1.0625$, $1.125$, $1.25$, $1.5$, $2$, $3$ and $5$. In this comparison mixing in real data gives a smaller
perplexity increase and keeps diversity near its starting range (Figure~\ref{fig:tempbracket}).

\subsection{Tokenizer composition and script drift}

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

Throughout, $C(p)=\sum_i p_i^2$ and $\Htwo=-\log_2 C$; logarithms are base 2 unless written $\ln$.

\subsection{Tempering, sampling and the scope of the crossing illustration}
\label{app:crossings}

These are the statements behind \S\ref{sec:crossings}. Fix a distribution $q$ on a finite alphabet with $q_i>0$, and write
\[
\psi(\beta)\;=\;\ln\sum_i q_i^{\beta},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad \psi(1)=0,\qquad H_\alpha(q)=\frac{\psi(\alpha)}{(1-\alpha)\ln2}\ \text{ bits}.
\]
$\psi$ is convex, being a log-sum-exp of the linear functions $\beta\mapsto\beta\ln q_i$. Let $q^{(\beta)}_i=q_i^\beta/\sum_j q_j^\beta$ denote the escort distribution at $\beta$, and recall the standard identities $\psi'(\beta)=\mathbb{E}_{q^{(\beta)}}[\ln q]$ and $\psi''(\beta)=\operatorname{Var}_{q^{(\beta)}}(\ln q)\ge0$.

\begin{proposition}[Tempering acts by dilation]
\label{prop:temper}
For every finite $\alpha\ne1$ and $\beta>0$,
\[
H_\alpha\!\left(q^{(\beta)}\right)\;=\;\frac{\psi(\alpha\beta)-\alpha\,\psi(\beta)}{(1-\alpha)\ln2},
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
H_1\!\left(q^{(\beta)}\right)\;=\;[\psi(\beta)-\beta\,\psi'(\beta)]/\ln2.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\sum_i\big(q_i^\beta/\sum_j q_j^\beta\big)^\alpha=\big(\sum_i q_i^{\alpha\beta}\big)\big/\big(\sum_j q_j^{\beta}\big)^{\alpha}$. Taking $\ln$ gives $\psi(\alpha\beta)-\alpha\psi(\beta)$, and dividing by $(1-\alpha)\ln2$ gives the first claim; $\beta=1$ recovers $H_\alpha(q)$ since $\psi(1)=0$. For the second, apply L'H\^opital's rule in $\alpha$ at $\alpha=1$: the numerator's derivative is $\beta\psi'(\alpha\beta)-\psi(\beta)$ and the denominator's is $-\ln2$. At $\beta=1$ this is $-\psi'(1)/\ln2=-\sum_i q_i\log_2 q_i$, the Shannon entropy, as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Temperature sensitivity]
\label{prop:sens}
Let $G(\alpha)=-\partial_\beta H_\alpha(q^{(\beta)})\big|_{\beta=1}$, the rate at which sharpening reduces $H_\alpha$. Then $G(\alpha)\ge0$ for all $\alpha\ge0$, with
\[
G(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{\ln2}\frac{\psi'(\alpha)-\psi'(1)}{\alpha-1}
=\frac{\alpha}{\ln2}\int_0^1\psi''(1+s(\alpha-1))\,ds,
\]
with $G(1)=\operatorname{Var}_q(\ln q)/\ln2$ by continuity, and $G(0)=0$; as $\alpha\to\infty$, $G(\alpha)\to H_1(q)-H_\infty(q)\ge0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiating Proposition~\ref{prop:temper} gives
$\partial_\beta H_\alpha=\alpha[\psi'(\alpha\beta)-\psi'(\beta)]/((1-\alpha)\ln2)$.
The divided difference of $\psi'$ equals the displayed integral. Since
$\psi''=\Var_{q^{(\beta)}}(\ln q)\ge0$, $G\ge0$. At order zero, tempering preserves the set of
positive probabilities, so population $H_0$ is constant. Finally, $\psi'(\alpha)\to\ln\max_i q_i$ as
$\alpha\to\infty$; substituting in the divided difference gives the stated limit.
\end{proof}

\emph{Recursive tempering.} For non-uniform $q$, $\psi''>0$, so the derivative above is negative at every $\beta>0$ for finite $\alpha>0$, $\alpha\ne1$. The limiting orders satisfy $\partial_\beta H_1=-\beta\psi''(\beta)/\ln2<0$ and $\partial_\beta H_\infty=[\psi'(\beta)-\ln\max_i q_i]/\ln2<0$. Since $(q^{(\beta)})^{(\beta')}=q^{(\beta\beta')}$, $g$ untruncated infinite-sample steps at fixed temperature $T$ give $q^{(T^{-g})}$ for every integer $g\ge1$. Relative to $q$, every positive-order entropy rises for $T>1$, falls for $0<T<1$ and is unchanged at $T=1$, while $H_0$ stays fixed.

\begin{proposition}[Collision bias and expected occupancy]
\label{prop:rarefy}
For $N\ge2$ i.i.d.\ draws from $q$, with counts $n_i$, empirical distribution $\hat q$ and $\widehat C=C(\hat q)$,
\[
\E\hat C=C+\frac{1-C}{N},\qquad
\E\!\left[\frac{\sum_i n_i(n_i-1)}{N(N-1)}\right]=C,
\qquad C=\sum_i q_i^2.
\]
Consequently $H_2(q)-\E H_2(\hat q)\le\log_2(1+(1-C)/(NC))$ by Jensen's inequality, which leaves
the sign of the deficit undetermined. For the observed support $S_N$,
$\E S_N=\sum_i[1-(1-q_i)^N]$ \citep[Eq.~14]{hurlbert1971}.

For a triangular sequence $q^{(N)}$ on $K_N\to\infty$ types (an alphabet size, unrelated to
Yule's $K$), suppose a fraction $\phi\in(0,1]$ of the types satisfy $c_1/N\le q_i\le c_2/N$ with
$0<c_1\le c_2<\infty$, and the remaining types (if any) satisfy
$\min_{i\,\mathrm{heavy}}Nq_i/\ln K_N\to\infty$. Then
\[
\phi K_Ne^{-c_2}(1+o(1))\le\E(K_N-S_N)\le \phi K_Ne^{-c_1}(1+o(1)).
\]
When $c_1=c_2=c$, $\log_2 K_N-\log_2\E S_N\to-\log_2(1-\phi e^{-c})$. The expected number of missing types
is $\Theta(K_N)$, whereas this logarithmic deficit is $\Theta(1)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Binomial marginals give $\E n_i^2=Nq_i(1-q_i)+N^2q_i^2$ and
$\E[n_i(n_i-1)]=N(N-1)q_i^2$, which prove both collision identities.
Convexity of $-\log_2$ gives the bound, and summing occupancy indicators gives $\E S_N$.
For light types, $(1-q_i)^N=e^{-Nq_i}(1+O(Nq_i^2))$ uniformly, so their probabilities of being
missing lie between $e^{-c_2}$ and $e^{-c_1}$ up to a factor $1+o(1)$. Each heavy type is absent
with probability at most $e^{-Nq_i}=K_N^{-\omega(1)}$, so the heavy types contribute $o(1)$ in
total. Summing proves the bound, and with $c_1=c_2=c$,
$\E S_N=K_N(1-\phi e^{-c}+o(1))$, which proves the limit.
\end{proof}

The logarithmic deficit above differs from the plug-in deficit $\log_2 K_N-\E\log_2 S_N$, which
is at least as large by Jensen's inequality. Under the proposition's assumptions the difference tends to zero.
Writing $\mu_N=\E S_N=\Theta(K_N)$, occupancy indicators have nonpositive pairwise covariance, so $\Var
S_N\le\mu_N$. Chebyshev's inequality gives $\Pr(|S_N-\mu_N|>\mu_N/2)=O(K_N^{-1})$. On the complementary event,
$|\ln(S_N/\mu_N)|\le2|S_N/\mu_N-1|$ has expectation $O(K_N^{-1/2})$; on the remaining event, $1\le
S_N,\mu_N\le K_N$ bounds its contribution by $O(\ln K_N/K_N)$. Thus the two richness deficits have the same
limit when $c_1=c_2$. Appendix~\ref{app:rarefy-fact} computes both. The lower mass bound is essential: with $K_N=N^2$
and $q_i=N^{-2}$, $\E S_N=\Theta(N)$ and the logarithmic deficit grows as $(1+o(1))\log_2 N$.
Population support cannot be recovered exactly from a finite sample without further assumptions
about unseen types \citep{good1953,chao1984}. When $\phi<1$, the heavy-type condition forces
$K_N\ln K_N/N\to0$, so the total mass of light types is $O(K_N/N)\to0$; the proposition
does not describe a tail of fixed positive mass in that case.

The logarithm introduces bias even when the collision-probability estimator is unbiased. For
$q=(1/2,1/2)$ and $N=2$, the plug-in entropy deficit is $0.5$ bits while its Jensen bound is
$\log_2(1.5)=0.585$, and the U-statistic can be zero, which makes its logarithm infinite. On a
measured English generation-0 law (the seed-0 baseline of the main grid, $\Htwo=6.96$ bits) at
$N=13{,}000$, the bound is $0.0137$ bits, the delta-method deficit is $0.0131$, and a
4,000-draw Monte Carlo estimate is $0.0128\pm0.0006$ bits (standard error). The logarithm of the U-statistic has a
residual bias estimated at $+0.0008\pm0.0006$ bits (standard error). For this non-degenerate law, $N$ exceeds
the support size, so the U-statistic is positive and nonconstant; strict Jensen inequality
makes its entropy bias strictly positive, despite Monte Carlo uncertainty. The
richness deficit on the same law is $1.69$ bits.

\paragraph{Limits of a first-order crossing explanation.} Suppose each generation lost a fixed
amount $L_\alpha$ of order-$\alpha$ entropy through finite sampling and gained $G(\alpha)(1-1/T)$
through tempering, the first-order expansion of Proposition~\ref{prop:sens}. The balance
$1-1/T^\ast=L_\alpha/G(\alpha)$ then puts any crossing above one whenever both loss and
sensitivity are positive, so it cannot produce the measured roots below one for $H_1$, whose
i.i.d.\ plug-in loss is always positive, or for $\Htwo$, whose loss is positive for large $N$
(Appendix~\ref{app:pluginsign}). A root below one requires a negative loss, which can occur at
higher orders: writing $\hat H_\alpha=H_\alpha(\hat q)$ for the plug-in estimate, exact binomial averaging for $q=(0.9,0.1)$ and $N=100$ gives
$\E\hat H_2-H_2=-0.0007285$, $\E\hat H_3-H_3=+0.0008191$ and $\E\hat H_\infty-H_\infty=+0.0008076$
bits, so orders 3 and $\infty$ gain entropy from sampling alone. Such a gain could place
$T^\ast(\Hinf)$ below one, but endpoint sensitivities do not establish a monotone ratio of loss
to gain for the intermediate orders. The
neural result is therefore empirical, and the categorical simulation provides one example of the
same outer ordering.

\paragraph{A categorical simulation.} We simulate a categorical model that alternates tempering,
13,000 draws and refitting
for nine generations. It pads the alphabet to 50,257 types, mixes each refit with a uniform
smoothing term of weight $0.02$, and measures with 13,000 further draws from a unigram analogue of the neural grid's top-$p=0.95$ measurement
sampler. Across 16 replicates the roots are $0.993$, $0.994$, $1.005$ and $1.129$ for orders
$\infty$, 2, 1 and 0. The simulation reproduces the outer ordering, with richness turning well
after the other orders, but the $\Hinf$ and $\Htwo$ roots ($0.9934$ and $0.9937$) lie in the same
grid bracket, $[0.95,1.00]$, so their order is not resolved. It does not predict the neural
roots, and without smoothing its richness does not recover in the tested range. That need not
hold in general, since a fixed nucleus sampler can expose previously excluded types even though the fitted support cannot grow (see the two-type example below). In detail, the generation-0 law is
the unsmoothed unigram law of the English generation-0 text used above; each generation tempers the current smoothed law, draws 13,000
tokens, and refits by maximum likelihood before mixing in the uniform term; and the measurement
sampler applies top-$p=0.95$ to the current smoothed law. The 14 temperatures run from $0.70$ to $2.00$
($0.70$, $0.80$, $0.90$, $0.95$, $1.00$, $1.02$, $1.05$, $1.10$, $1.20$, $1.30$, $1.45$, $1.60$,
$1.80$ and $2.00$), and replicate $i$ uses the same random seed at every temperature.

Population support and the support seen by a fixed sampler must also be distinguished. Take $q=(0.9999,0.0001)$, untruncated
training at $T=2$, $N=13{,}000$ and unsmoothed categorical refitting. A fixed top-$p=0.95$ sampler initially keeps
only the first type. Tempering can raise the second type's mass until it enters the nucleus, so
observed $H_0$ can increase without adding any type outside the original population support. A
nine-generation simulation does so in all 100 tested seeds. Thus $G(0)=0$ does not imply that
richness under a fixed sampler cannot recover under maximum-likelihood refitting.

\subsection{Unbiased estimators for integer-order power sums}
\label{app:factorial-estimator}
For integers $N\ge m\ge2$, write $(a)_m=a(a-1)\cdots(a-m+1)$. Under multinomial sampling, an
unbiased estimator of $P_m(q)=\sum_i q_i^m$ is
\[
\widehat P_{m,\mathrm u}=\frac{\sum_i(n_i)_m}{(N)_m},
\qquad \E\widehat P_{m,\mathrm u}=P_m(q).
\]
Indeed, $(n_i)_m$ counts ordered tuples of $m$ distinct sample positions that all contain type
$i$. Each tuple has probability $q_i^m$, so $\E(n_i)_m=(N)_m q_i^m$, and summing over types
proves the identity. It is the fixed-sample counterpart of the bias-corrected estimator of
\citet{acharya2017renyi}: for Poisson sample size $M\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(N)$, their numerator
$\sum_i(n_i)_m$ is divided by $N^m$, the $m$th power of the expected sample size. At order 2
the fixed-sample estimator above is the pair-count estimator. Applying the nonlinear entropy transform
$\log_2\widehat P_{m,\mathrm u}/(1-m)$ requires a separate bias analysis, and a zero estimate
gives infinite entropy.

\subsection{Order monotonicity \texorpdfstring{$H_0\ge H_1\ge \Htwo\ge H_\infty$}{H0 >= H1 >= H2 >= H infinity}}
Let $P_\alpha=\sum_i p_i^\alpha$ and $\pi_i=p_i^\alpha/P_\alpha$ (the escort distribution). In
bits,
$\frac{d H_\alpha}{d\alpha}=\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)^2\ln2}\big[\ln P_\alpha+(1-\alpha)\tfrac{P_\alpha'}{P_\alpha}\big]$
with $P_\alpha'=\sum_i p_i^\alpha\ln p_i$. Substituting $\ln P_\alpha=\alpha\ln p_i-\ln\pi_i$
reduces the bracket to $\sum_i\pi_i\ln(p_i/\pi_i)=-\ln(2)\,\KL(\pi\Vert p)\le0$. The continuous limits
cover orders $1$ and $\infty$, and zero-probability entries are omitted from the support. Hence
$H_\alpha$ is non-increasing in $\alpha$. $\square$

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{thm:temp} (temperature monotonicity)}
With $p_i=e^{\beta z_i}/Z$, $Z=\sum_j e^{\beta z_j}$ and $\mu=\sum_j p_j z_j$, the quotient rule gives $\frac{dp_i}{d\beta}=p_i(z_i-\mu)$, so
$\frac{d}{d\beta}\sum_i p_i^2=2\sum_i p_i^2(z_i-\mu)=2\Cov(p_I,z_I)$, where $I\sim p$. Taking an independent copy $I'\sim p$,
\[
2\Cov(p_I,z_I)=\E\big[(p_I-p_{I'})(z_I-z_{I'})\big].
\]
Since $p_i$ is strictly increasing in $z_i$ for $\beta>0$, the two factors share a sign, so the product is nonnegative pointwise and its expectation is nonnegative, and strictly positive unless $z$ is constant. Thus $\sum_i p_i^2$ strictly increases in $\beta$ and strictly decreases in $T$, and $\Htwo$ strictly increases in $T$. The boundary limits follow by factoring out $e^{\beta z_{\max}}$ (so $\sum_i p_i^2\to1/m$ and $\Htwo\to\log_2 m$ as $\beta\to\infty$) and from $p_i\to1/|\V|$ as $\beta\to0$, which requires finite logits ($\Htwo\to\log_2|\V|$). $\square$

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{thm:trunc} and Lemma~\ref{lem:upperset}}
\begin{lemma}[Upper-set conditioning]
\label{lem:upperset}
With $X=p_I$, $I\sim p$, and $S$ an upper set of mass $0<a<1$, $\E[X\mid I\in S]\ge\E[X]$; equality holds precisely when every retained $p_i$ and every strictly positive discarded $p_j$ share a common value.
\end{lemma}
\emph{Proof.} Let $A=\sum_{i\in S}p_i^2$, $a=\sum_{i\in S}p_i$, and let $B,b$ be the corresponding sums over the complement. Then $\E[X\mid I\in S]=A/a$, $\E[X\mid I\notin S]=B/b$ and $\E[X]=A+B$. Because $S$ is an upper set, $A/a\ge\min_{i\in S}p_i\ge\max_{j\notin S}p_j\ge B/b$. By total expectation, $\E[X]=a\,\E[X\mid I\in S]+b\,\E[X\mid I\notin S]$, and subtracting gives $\E[X\mid I\in S]-\E[X]=b\big(\E[X\mid I\in S]-\E[X\mid I\notin S]\big)\ge0$. Equality forces equality
throughout this chain. Because the weights $p_i/a$ are positive on $S$ and the weights $p_j/b$ are positive
exactly on the strictly positive discarded entries, $A/a=\min_{i\in S}p_i$ makes all retained $p_i$ equal,
$\max_{j\notin S}p_j=B/b$ makes all strictly positive discarded $p_j$ equal, and the middle link makes the two
values coincide; the converse is immediate. $\square$

\emph{Proposition~\ref{thm:trunc}.} With $q_i=p_i/a$ on $S$,
\[
\sum_{i\in S}q_i^2=\tfrac{1}{a^2}\sum_{i\in S}p_i^2=\tfrac1a\E[X\mid I\in S]\ge\tfrac1a\E[X]>\E[X]=\sum_i p_i^2.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:upperset} gives the first, weak inequality, and the factor $1/a>1$ makes the second strict. Applying $-\log_2(\cdot)$ reverses the inequality, so $\Htwo(q)<\Htwo(p)$. Without the upper-set condition the result fails: keeping the two smaller types of $p=(0.9,0.05,0.05)$ raises $\Htwo$ from $0.295$ to $1$ bit. $\square$

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:cap} (support cap)}
For $q$ supported on $S$ with $|S|=k$, the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives $1=(\sum_{i\in S}q_i)^2\le k\sum_{i\in S}q_i^2$, so $\sum_{i\in S}q_i^2\ge1/k$ and $\Htwo(q)\le\log_2 k$, with equality if and only if $q$ is uniform on $S$. No reshaping of mass within $S$, at any temperature, can exceed $\log_2 k$. $\square$

\subsection{Lemma~\ref{lem:vbridge} (the \texorpdfstring{$V$}{V}-bridge)}
For a uniformly chosen position $t$, $\Var_t[p_{c_t}(v)]=\overline{p_{c_t}(v)^2}-\pbar(v)^2$.
Summing over $v$ gives $C(\pbar)=\Cbar-V$ exactly, with $V\ge0$. $\square$

Equivalently, $1-C(\pbar)=(1-\Cbar)+V$: pooled Gini--Simpson diversity is the sum
of its mean within-context value and the between-context variance \citep{rao1982,lande1996}.
This additive partition differs from a decomposition into independent diversity components
in the sense of \citet{jost2007}.

For the fixed-length path statement, let $X_t$ be the one-hot sampled token, $\mathcal F_{t-1}$
the history up to position $t-1$, and $p_t=\E[X_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]$ its sampling law. The
differences $X_t-p_t$ are martingale differences, so their cross inner products have zero
expectation. Hence
\[
\E\left\|\ph_N-\pbar_N\right\|_2^2
=\frac{1}{N^2}\sum_{t=1}^N\E[1-\|p_t\|_2^2]\le\frac1N.
\]
This requires the conditionals of the actual sampler, including any truncation; softmaxes on
unrelated human contexts do not qualify. The bound alone gives neither an i.i.d.\ entropy-bias
formula for the path nor a fixed limiting distribution.

\subsection{Plug-in bias, with its sign}\label{app:pluginsign} For $N$ i.i.d.\ draws, $f\sim\mathrm{Multinomial}(N,p)$ with $\mathrm{Binomial}(N,p_v)$ marginals, $\E[\ph_v^2]=p_v^2+p_v(1-p_v)/N$, so $\E[\widehat C]=C+(1-C)/N$. For independent but non-identical conditionals the same calculation gives the $V$-bridge form $\E\,C(\ph)=C(\pbar)+(1-\Cbar)/N$. For comparison, \citet{miller1955} and \citet{paninski2003} study bias in Shannon-entropy estimation. To determine the sign of the induced bias in $\Htwo$, write $P_3=\sum_v p_v^3$. A delta-method expansion of $\hat\Htwo=-\log_2\widehat C$, using $\Var\,\widehat C=\tfrac4N(P_3-C^2)+O(N^{-2})$, gives
\[
\E\,\hat\Htwo-\Htwo=-\frac{C+C^2-2P_3}{N\,C^2\ln 2}+O(N^{-2}).
\]
The remainder is controlled because $1/|\operatorname{supp}p|\le\widehat C\le1$, so $-\log_2$ and
its derivatives are bounded on the range of $\widehat C$, and the moments about $C$,
$\E(\widehat C-C)^j$ are $O(N^{-2})$ for $j=3,4$; a fourth-order Taylor expansion with Lagrange
remainder then gives the stated error, with constants that depend on $p$. The numerator is strictly
positive for non-degenerate $p$: from $P_3=\sum_v p_v\,p_v^2\le p_{\max}C$ and $C\ge p_{\max}^2$,
\[
C+C^2-2P_3\ \ge\ C\big(1+C-2p_{\max}\big)\ \ge\ C\big(1-p_{\max}\big)^2\ >\ 0
\]
whenever $p_{\max}<1$. Hence, for all sufficiently large $N$, $\E\,\hat\Htwo<\Htwo$: the
collision-rate bias dominates the competing Jensen term, and the plug-in $\Htwo$ is biased
\emph{downward}, which supports the asymptotic entropy form of the ratchet in
Proposition~\ref{prop:ratchet}. $\square$

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:ratchet} (diversity ratchet)}
\begin{proposition}[Truncated categorical refitting is a diversity ratchet]
\label{prop:ratchet}
Idealize generation $g{+}1$ as the categorical maximum-likelihood estimate (the empirical distribution) $\ph_g$ of $N$ draws from the top-$p$ truncation $q_g$ ($a<1$) of generation $g$'s law $p_g$. Then $\E\big[2^{-\Htwo(\ph_g)}\mid p_g\big]>2^{-\Htwo(p_g)}$ for every $N$.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Proof.} Truncation gives $C(q_g)>C(p_g)$ (Proposition~\ref{thm:trunc}), and
categorical refitting gives
\[
\E[C(\ph_g)\mid q_g]=C(q_g)+\frac{1-C(q_g)}{N}\ge C(q_g)>C(p_g).
\]
This proves the collision inequality for every $N$. For the entropy form, fix $p_g$ and its
retained set. The expansion above gives $\E[\Htwo(\ph_g)\mid q_g]=\Htwo(q_g)+O(N^{-1})$, and since
$\Htwo(q_g)<\Htwo(p_g)$, the expected entropy also decreases for sufficiently large $N$. The
entropy conclusion is asymptotic, whereas the collision inequality is exact. $\square$

Without truncation ($a=1$, $q_g=p_g$) the collision inequality still holds, since
$\E[C(\ph_g)\mid p_g]=C(p_g)+(1-C(p_g))/N>C(p_g)$ for non-degenerate $p_g$, and the sign result of
Appendix~\ref{app:pluginsign} shows that expected $\Htwo$ also falls for large $N$, by $O(N^{-1})$
rather than by the fixed amount that truncation removes. The rise of the neural untruncated arms
(\S\ref{sec:robust}) lies outside this model.

\subsection{Entropy and reference cross-perplexity}
For one categorical law, $2^{H_1(p)}\ge2^{H_2(p)}$ by order monotonicity. Cross-perplexity on a
reference law $r$ is $2^{H(r,p)}$, where $H(r,p)=H(r)+\KL(r\Vert p)$ in bits, which is finite if
and only if $r\ll p$ on a finite alphabet. The two quantities can move in opposite directions: for
uniform $r$ on $K>1$ types and $p_\epsilon=(1-(K-1)\epsilon,\epsilon,\ldots,\epsilon)$, as
$\epsilon\downarrow0$, $H_1(p_\epsilon)\to0$ while $H(r,p_\epsilon)\to\infty$.

independently. Half of $K$ types have mass $1/N$ and the rest share the remaining mass. Choosing
$N=\lfloor K(\ln K)^2\rfloor$ makes the required ratio $\min Nq_i/\ln K$ over heavy types grow with $K$
(Table~\ref{tab:factorial}).

\subsection{Finite-sample estimator checks}
\label{app:rarefy-emp}

We compare the finite-sample estimators of Appendix~\ref{app:crossings} on known distributions
\citep[cf.][]{chao2012coverage,chao2014rarefaction}.

\paragraph{Estimator comparisons.} A uniform law on 5,000 types, Zipf laws on 20,000 types ($s=0.8$, $1.0$ and $1.2$) and measured endpoint laws
are sampled at budgets from $N/8$ to $2N$, with $N=13{,}000$. On the English generation-0 law of
Appendix~\ref{app:crossings} at $N=13{,}000$, the mean pair-count collision estimate over 4,000
draws is $8.0166\times10^{-3}$ against a true value of $8.0181\times10^{-3}$. The plug-in
entropy deficit is $0.0128\pm0.0006$ bits (standard error), against the Jensen bound of $0.0137$ and the
delta-method approximation of $0.0131$, and the log U-statistic retains $+0.0008\pm0.0006$ bits (standard error); its population bias is strictly positive by the Jensen argument above. Observed
richness instead has a $1.69$-bit deficit at 31\% type coverage, and no collision correction
estimates unseen types.

Low type coverage alone does not determine the accuracy of the delta-method correction. Its
collision-rate shift relative to $C$ is $(1-C)/(NC)$, so small $NC$ can make the expansion
poor; higher moments also enter the entropy correction. On the uniform law at $N=1{,}625$
($NC=0.33$, 28\% type coverage), it predicts a $4.44$-bit deficit against a measured $2.03$,
an error of $2.41$ bits. The error falls to $0.88$ at $NC=0.65$ and $0.09$ at $NC=2.6$.
Conversely, on the Zipf law with $s=1.2$ at $N=1{,}625$, the delta-method prediction matches the Monte Carlo
deficit to within its standard error (a difference of $0.0004$ bits against a standard error of $0.0015$) at
2.4\% type coverage ($NC=94$). Coverage and the estimated collision scale $N\,2^{-\widehat H_2}$ should both be
reported; neither low coverage nor $NC$ alone is a universal accuracy criterion.

\paragraph{The reported $\Hz$ and $\Htwo$ changes are large relative to seed variability.} For each cell on the
six-temperature top-$p=1$ axis, we divide the mean common-length change from generation~0 to
generation~9 by its standard error across the five seeds, a one-sample $t$ statistic that treats
seeds as the independent units, since generations within a trajectory are not independent.
Observed changes sit $10.5$ to $442$ standard errors from zero for $\Htwo$ (median $49$) and $11.3$ to $89$ for $\Hz$ (median $51$).
These ratios quantify variability across training seeds, whereas same-law rarefaction resamples
a fixed distribution. Separate generation-0 sampling controls, compared at each pair's common
length (about 70,000 words), give a largest movement of $0.061$ bits at any of the four primary orders and a
two-point profile statistic below $0.049$. Calibrating the 13,000-word alarm rules requires controls at that smaller budget. For comparison, the $T=1$ changes in
Table~\ref{tab:crossings} are $-0.81$ bits for $H_0$ and $+0.94$ for $H_2$. An autoregressive bias correction remains a separate problem from these sampling controls and
the $0.013$-bit i.i.d.\ reference calculation.


\subsection{Post-hoc checks of order, head concentration and measurement scale}
\label{app:posthocanalyses}

The following analyses use the stored English endpoints and count multisets and, for the detection baselines,
the released 660-row feature pool; none trains a language model. They were developed after inspecting the main results. Saved outputs and analysis provenance
are released in \texttt{code/exp\_data/posthoc\_analyses/}, with scripts in
\texttt{code/scripts/posthoc\_analyses/}.

\paragraph{An interpolation-free sign statement.} We evaluate
\[
\alpha\in\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5,0.75,1,1.25,1.5,2,2.5,3,4,5,7,10,20,50,\infty\}
\]
on the eleven-temperature, top-$p=1$ English axis. At each measured temperature the changes
are either all negative, all positive, or pass once from negative to positive as order increases,
in every seed and in the mean, under both common-length and 13,000-word budgets. This is a
statement about the sampled orders; it does not assert monotonicity of the magnitudes or a
theorem for every real order. The interpolated crossing temperatures decrease strictly across
all 18 orders in 4,000/4,000 common-length bootstrap draws and 3,976/4,000 (99.4\%) fixed-budget
draws. The four primary orders retain their ordering in all 4,000 under both budgets.

\paragraph{Head decomposition.} Let $A_k$ be the $k$ most frequent types in the pooled five
generation-0 texts. For each seed we truncate generation~0 and~9 to common length, decompose
$C=\sum_{w\in A_k}\hat p_w^2+\sum_{w\notin A_k}\hat p_w^2$, and report the mean across seeds
of the fraction of the total change attributable to $A_k$. At $T=1$, $A_{10}$ consists of
\emph{the, to, and, of, a, in, i, that, it, he}; it accounts for 104\% of the fall in $C$,
while $A_{50}$ accounts for 108\%. Contributions above 100\% mean the remaining types offset
part of the decrease. Mean $C$ falls from $0.00784$ to $0.00410$, and the share of \emph{the}
falls from 4.94\% to 1.81\%; the total share of $A_{50}$ falls from 43.55\% to 30.88\%.
At generation~9, \emph{to} is most frequent in four seeds and \emph{of} in one; the mean of these maximum frequencies is 2.63\%.
After removing $A_{50}$ from both texts and matching the lengths of the retained streams again,
the changes in $(H_0,H_1,H_2,H_\infty)$ are $(-0.972,-0.969,-0.390,-0.686)$ bits.
This descriptive analysis selects sets by frequency and renormalizes the distribution after
deletion. Causal and grammatical effects require separate tests.

\paragraph{Sample coverage.} Coverage is estimated probability mass of observed types, distinct

of its two estimated coverages by coverage-based rarefaction
\citep{chao2012coverage,chao2014rarefaction} gives mean richness change $-1.91$ bits, against

68,835 and 15,375 words. Coverage is estimated under exchangeable counts and continuous
interpolation of rarefaction sizes; accuracy for the underlying autoregressive process remains
untested.

\paragraph{Phrase spectra.} Common-length bigram roots for $(H_0,H_1,H_2,H_\infty)$ are

\paragraph{Simple detection baselines.} On the same 660 feature rows and 14 two-class

folds. Adding generation index to the $(H_0,H_2)$ logistic model gives 0.991. Unsigned richness scores higher than the two fixed pairs in this pool.
Appendix~\ref{app:familyholdout} gives a post-hoc family-holdout analysis; prospective transfer
to additional model families and the mechanisms behind these scores remain untested. The pooled in-sample $(H_0,H_2)$ fit has AUROC 0.909, with a
block-bootstrap interval $[0.879,0.945]$ over 48 (setting, seed) blocks.

\subsection{Matched controls under the primary measurement protocol}
\label{app:freshcontrols}

five unchanged-model controls with the same seed indices. At each generation, a human
adaptation draws fresh blocks with replacement from the original five-book training pool.
Its paired recursive run determines the number of 256-token blocks and optimizer updates;
learning rate, batch size, epochs, optimizer reset and gradient clipping are identical.
Human block sampling has a separate recorded random-number stream. All three arms share
the prompt pool and keyed measurement streams, and all entropies use the first 13,000 words.
The frozen arm changes only the measurement seed across generations and takes no optimizer steps.

unfloored rule from \S\ref{sec:detect}. A separately specified $0.02$-bit floor tests sensitivity
