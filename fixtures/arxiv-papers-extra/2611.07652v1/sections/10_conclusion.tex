\section{Conclusion: The Discipline of Capability Engineering}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The first era of generative artificial intelligence was defined by empirical wonder: discovering that massive transformers trained on internet-scale text could converse, reason, and simulate diverse personae. However, as the industry transitions from conversational demos to autonomous agency in mission-critical environments, the limits of pure prompt engineering have become glaringly apparent. 

Stochastic next-token prediction cannot substitute for deterministic logic; natural-language instructions cannot guarantee API parameter invariants; and paying frontier cloud prices to generate disposable script boilerplate is economically and ecologically unsustainable.

In this work, we have presented \textbf{Skillware}, an open architectural framework and capability registry that restores engineering rigor to autonomous systems. By formally decoupling \emph{cognitive intent} from \emph{functional capability}, Skillware replaces fragile, prompt-driven tool execution with self-contained, versioned, and pre-compiled software capability bundles. 

Through its five atomic roles---Contract, Effect, Directive, Assurance, and Presentation---Skillware guarantees typed inputs, enforces hard constitutional invariants, eliminates prompt bloat, and provides zero-latency in-process interface adaptation across Google Gemini, Anthropic Claude, OpenAI, DeepSeek, AWS Bedrock, and local Ollama runtimes.

We have demonstrated that the path to resilient, sovereign, and cost-effective autonomy does not lie in endlessly scaling model parameters. Small Language Models (3B--8B parameters) running locally on commodity hardware, when equipped with deterministic Skillware bundles, achieve higher operational reliability, faster execution speeds, and a \textbf{97.3\% cost reduction} over prompt-driven frontier models. 

Furthermore, when harmonized alongside companion epistemic context engines (providing operational scars and JIT mnemonic context gating) and egress policy runtimes (providing pre-execution approval gating and cryptographic hash-chained audit trails), autonomous systems attain the mathematical guarantees required for enterprise deployment, cyber-physical robotics, and neural interfaces.

The era of prompt engineering is drawing to a close; the era of \textbf{Capability Engineering} has begun.

\section*{Availability and Open Science}
Skillware is free, open-source software distributed under the MIT license. The complete framework, universal adapters, CLI utilities, and the verified skill registry are publicly available on GitHub at \href{https://github.com/arpahls/skillware}{\texttt{https://github.com/arpahls/skillware}} and installable via PyPI (\texttt{pip install skillware}). 

The permanent concept DOI for citing this framework is \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21552745}{\texttt{10.5281/zenodo.21552745}} (software release v0.6.0: \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23620979}{\texttt{10.5281/zenodo.23620979}}~\cite{skillware2026framework}). The companion \textsc{Skillware-Bench} evaluation suite is archived under \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23726128}{\texttt{10.5281/zenodo.23726128}} (v0.3.3)~\cite{peilivanidis2026skillwarebench}, and the AURA Harness runtime coat is archived under \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23411947}{\texttt{10.5281/zenodo.23411947}} (v0.3.6)~\cite{aura2026harness}. Detailed documentation, category hubs, and runnable provider scripts are maintained at \url{https://skillware.site}.
