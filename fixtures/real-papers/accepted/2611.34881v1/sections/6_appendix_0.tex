\section{STRANGE-Bench: Pipeline Details and Statistics}
\label{app:strange-pipeline}

STRANGE-Bench is our diffusion-generated tiered evaluation set: $5{,}523$ photorealistic scenes with $17{,}332$ instance annotations across $17$ NPMI-stratified sub-tiers, spanning typical, atypical, and never-co-occurring COCO object pairs. The remainder of this section describes the dataset release (\S\ref{app:strange:release}), shows qualitative samples (\S\ref{app:strange:samples}), reports the per-tier composition and sanity diagnostics (\S\ref{app:strange:tiers}--\S\ref{app:strange:diag}), describes the five-stage generation pipeline (\S\ref{app:strange:pipeline}), and lists the implementation hyperparameters
(\S\ref{app:strange:impl}).

\label{app:strange:release}

\noindent\textbf{Variants.}
We release two variants: the \emph{balanced} variant ($5{,}523$ images / $17{,}332$ instance annotations) is the configuration whose numbers we report throughout this paper; the pre-balancing \emph{clean} variant ($6{,}571$ / $21{,}349$) is included for transparency. We also release the IDs of the $3{,}234$ scenes in which no class appears in the generated training scenes of the AAS late stage (the held-out-class subset of App.~\ref{app:circularity}).

\noindent\textbf{Format.}
Images are $1024 \times 1024$ PNG; annotations follow the COCO instance format (bounding boxes + per-class segmentation masks).
A Croissant~1.1 file with both core and Responsible-AI~1.0 fields is shipped at the repository root.

\noindent\textbf{License.}
Data: \textbf{CC-BY 4.0}. Generation code: \textbf{MIT}. The dataset is intended for evaluation only; we ask users not to train on it.

\noindent\textbf{Reproducibility.}
The full pipeline, from the NPMI matrix through layout sampling, rendering, segmentation and the two audits to tier balancing, is reproducible from the included code; required external assets are public model weights and the COCO~\texttt{train2017} annotations.

\noindent\textbf{Generality and Extensibility.}
The pipeline above is deliberately recipe-driven and not COCO-specific. Swapping the input \emph{label vocabulary} (here, the $80$ COCO classes) and the source \emph{co-occurrence statistics} (here, NPMI on COCO~\texttt{train2017}) is sufficient to generate a STRANGE-Bench-style probe for any other dataset or task family. We release dataset, code, samples, and the Croissant manifest (Tab.~\ref{tab:strange_links}) under a permissive license so the community can:
\begin{itemize}

 \item regenerate STRANGE-Bench at higher resolution or larger scale, 
 \item target alternative vocabularies (LVIS~\cite{gupta2019lvis}, Objects365~\cite{shao2019objects365}, fine-grained categories, video frames), 
 \item substitute newer generative models (e.g., higher-resolution SD variants~\cite{stabilityai2024sd35}, Imagen~\cite{saharia2022photorealistic}, FLUX~\cite{flux2024}) or stronger auditors as they appear, and 
 \item compose with new typicality axes (action-object, scene-object, temporal context).
\end{itemize}
The pipeline's modular form (layout, render, segment, audit) means each stage can be replaced independently without rebuilding the rest. Our intent is to provide both a benchmark and a reusable construction recipe for stress-testing co-occurrence shortcuts in vision systems.

\subsection{Sub-Tier Composition}
\label{app:strange:tiers}

\paragraph{Two axes.}
STRANGE-Bench varies two axes: \emph{partner-count} $k\!\in\!\{0,\dots,5\}$ and \emph{pair-typicality} (typical: high-NPMI partners; atypical: low-NPMI or never-co-occurring partners; never-co-occurring: $N_{ij}\!=\!0$ for every pair of the scene, with the fallback below). The anchor-only setting ($k\!=\!0$) splits into a natural-background and an atypical-background variant, giving two sub-tiers at $k\!=\!0$, two each at $k\!=\!1,\dots,4$, and one \texttt{tier\_never} sub-tier for a total of eleven, the grid of the main tables. Six more sub-tiers appear only in App.~\ref{app:additional_results}: the typical and atypical pair at $k\!=\!5$ ($T_6$), a $k\!=\!1$ pair whose anchor box covers at most $4\%$ of the image, and never-co-occurring cliques with $k\!=\!3$ and $k\!=\!4$.

\paragraph{Anchor sampling and partner pools.}
Anchors are allocated evenly over the $80$ COCO categories ($74$ to $80$ anchors per sub-tier of the main grid after filtering). Partners are added one at a time, and each new partner must meet the arm's rule with the anchor and with every partner already chosen. The rule uses $A_{ij} = (1 - \text{NPMI}_{ij})/2$ on COCO~\texttt{train2017}: typical pairs need $A_{ij} \leq 0.45$ (NPMI $\geq 0.1$) and at least $10$ co-occurrences with the anchor; atypical pairs need $A_{ij} \geq 0.55$ (NPMI $\leq -0.1$). Pairs that never co-occur get $A_{ij} = 0.8$, above every co-occurring pair. Each partner is drawn by a softmax over the $15$ highest-scoring candidates; if no candidate meets the rule, the sampler takes the highest-scoring one. 

\paragraph{The \texttt{tier\_never} construction.}
For \texttt{tier\_never} we attempt a strict $3$-clique (every pair among $\{\text{anchor}, p_1, p_2\}$ has $N_{ij}\!=\!0$), or a pair when the anchor has a single never-co-occurring partner; for ubiquitous anchors that admit no $3$-clique (\emph{person}, \emph{car}, \emph{chair}, \dots), we pad the largest strict sub-clique with atypical partners. Of the $466$ \texttt{tier\_never} scenes ($397$ with two partners, $69$ with one), $83.7\%$ are strict cliques; the rest are fallback scenes whose strict-clique status can be derived from \texttt{labels\_intended} together with the COCO-train co-occurrence matrix. The cliques with $k\!=\!3$ and $k\!=\!4$ are strict by construction and use only pairs whose expected count under independence, $N p_i p_j$, is at least $10$, so that a zero count reflects absence rather than two rare classes; anchors without such a clique are skipped ($46$ and $28$ anchors remain). Per-tier composition is in Table~\ref{tab:strange_tiers}.

\subsection{Sanity Diagnostics}
\label{app:strange:diag}

Three checks test the typicality structure and two nuisance variables, object scale and position. \textit{(i) Tier semantics.} Per-tier intended-pair NPMI on COCO-train separates the typical and atypical sub-tiers at every quartile: the lower quartile of every typical sub-tier is at least $+0.05$ and the upper quartile of every atypical sub-tier at most $-0.20$ (typical means $+0.18$ to $+0.24$, atypical $-0.27$ to $-0.31$; pairs that never co-occur have no NPMI and are left out; Fig.~\ref{fig:npmi_per_tier}). \textit{(ii) Object-scale balance.} Median relative bbox area within typical/atypical pairs at fixed $k$ is matched to within $0.15$--$0.66$\,pt for $k\!=\!1,\dots,5$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:bbox_area_per_tier}); the largest gap is at $k\!=\!1$ ($0.66$\,pt), and the single anchor of $T_1$ is $3.0$\,pt larger on the natural than on the atypical background. \textit{(iii) Spatial layout.} Box centers sit below the image center on average in every sub-tier (mean vertical position $0.60$ to $0.67$ of the image height, $0$ at the top), and the typical and atypical sub-tiers at the same $k$ match within $0.013$ in mean position (Fig.~\ref{fig:spatial_centers}), so the two arms share the same center bias.

\subsection{Generation Pipeline}
\label{app:strange:pipeline}

For each scene we sample $k{+}1$ COCO categories from a target sub-tier, ask \textit{Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct}~\citep{qwen2024qwen25} for a layout (JSON bounding boxes, LayoutGPT-style~\citep{feng2023layoutgpt,lian2024lmd,cho2023vpgen}) and a photographic caption from those boxes' positions \citep{betker2023dalle3,hao2023promptist}, render with \textit{Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large}~\citep{stabilityai2024sd35,esser2024scaling}, segment with \textit{SAM3}~\citep{carion2026sam3},
and discard the scene if a \textit{Grounding DINO}~\citep{liu2024grounding} auditor flags extras or duplicates, or if the second audit or the completeness check of Stage 5 fails. Numeric hyperparameters for every stage are deferred to \ref{app:strange:impl}.

\noindent\textbf{Stage 1: layout and caption (Qwen).}
We use Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct twice per scene with the same weights but two fixed system+user templates. The \emph{layout call} (LayoutGPT-style) emits pixel-coordinate bounding boxes; the \emph{caption call} writes the photographic prompt for SD~3.5L from the boxes' positions and per-class visual descriptions. 

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Layout system.} \emph{You are a layout designer. Output
ONLY a JSON array of bounding boxes.}

\medskip
\textbf{Layout user.} \emph{Generate a realistic bounding box layout for an image of size $W\!\times\!H$. Objects to place:
\texttt{\{labels\}}. Return ONLY a JSON list where each item has
``label'' and ``bbox'' $[x_1, y_1, x_2, y_2]$ in pixels. Ensure
boxes don't overlap excessively and are realistically sized and
positioned. Each box must be inside the image
($0 \leq x_1 < x_2 \leq W$, $0 \leq y_1 < y_2 \leq H$). Each box
should be at least $64$ pixels wide and tall. Return labels exactly
as given. Example: $[\{$``label'': ``cat'', ``bbox'': $[50, 200,
250, 450]\}, \dots]$.}
\end{quote}

The emitted boxes are the \emph{planner layout boxes}; they reach the renderer only through the caption; on persistent JSON failure we fall back to a rule-based layout solver that places the boxes from COCO size priors without overlap. $T_6$ and the never-co-occurring cliques use the solver only; $79.2\%$ of the scenes have Qwen layouts. For the caption call we
discretize each box center $(c_x, c_y)/W \in [0,1]^2$ at
$\{0.33, 0.67\}$ to one of nine position strings
(Table~\ref{tab:position_strings}).

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Caption system.} \emph{You are an expert visual-art director writing prompts for a high-end photorealistic text-to-image model. Output ONLY the final prompt as a single sentence -- no preamble, no JSON.}

\medskip
\textbf{Caption user.} \emph{Write a single rich photographic prompt (one sentence, $30$--$70$ words) for a wide-shot photograph (full scene, not close-up) containing the following objects. The image is \texttt{\{context\_label\}}; the objects \texttt{\{tier\_phrase\}}.}

\smallskip
\emph{Objects (label $\rightarrow$ position $\rightarrow$ visual
description):}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item \texttt{\{label\}} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{\{position\}} within the image $\rightarrow$ \texttt{\{class\_visual\_description\}} \item \emph{...one entry per intended class}
\end{itemize}

\emph{The prompt MUST: be a wide / full shot; show every object completely (no cropping); mention each object by its exact label and spatial position; keep a minimalist scene (no extra people or unprompted clutter); specify environment, lighting, time of day, and mood; and use photorealistic-photography style words (wide angle, DSLR, 4K, sharp focus, natural light).}
\end{quote}

The \texttt{\{context\_label\}} and \texttt{\{tier\_phrase\}} fields are tier-conditioned (Table~\ref{tab:tier_phrases}). \texttt{\{class\_\allowbreak visual\_\allowbreak description\}} is a hand-written phrase per COCO class (e.g.\ ``a giraffe with very long neck and patterned coat''); the full $80$-class mapping is given in Table~\ref{tab:class_descriptions}. On total LLM failure we fall back to a deterministic template ``A photorealistic photo of a $\langle$label$_1\rangle$, \dots, and a $\langle$label$_n\rangle$, $\langle$bg-descriptor$\rangle$'' (loses spatial language but preserves the class set). The constraint list was hand-tuned across pilot runs to suppress two SD failure modes -- tight crops (headless figures, partial bodies) and unprompted clutter (extra people in the background).

\noindent\textbf{Stages 2-3: rendering and segmentation (SD~3.5L + SAM3).}
We render the caption with Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large up to three times under a fixed negative prompt covering crops, clutter, duplication, and quality artifacts. Per-class instance masks are extracted by SAM3 with text prompts of the form \texttt{``a \{label\}''}; a small set of overrides resolves ambiguous COCO names (\texttt{bed} $\to$ \texttt{``a picture of a bed''}, \texttt{keyboard} $\to$ \texttt{``a computer keyboard''}, \texttt{skis} $\to$ \texttt{``a pair of skis''}, \texttt{airplane} $\to$ \texttt{``an airplane''}, \texttt{hot\_dog} $\to$ \texttt{``a hot dog in a bun''}). SAM3 finds $89.5\%$ of the intended classes on one render and $95.9\%$ with up to three; we keep the first render in which it finds every intended class.

\noindent\textbf{Stage 4: consistency audit (Grounding DINO).}
Grounding DINO Swin-B runs over the COCO-$80$ vocabulary and applies two checks: (i)~\textit{extras}, any detected class above threshold not in the intended set;
(ii)~\textit{duplicates}, more than one detection of any intended class. Failing scenes are discarded.
The audit keeps $85.0\%$ of the renders at $k = 0$, $75.5$, $70.9$, $59.8$ and $51.0\%$ at $k = 1$ to $4$, and $46.5\%$ at $k = 5$; every rejected render has an extra or a duplicate object.

\noindent\textbf{Stage 5: second audit and balancing.} SAM3 and OWLv2~\cite{minderer2023scaling} run over the COCO-$80$ vocabulary at low thresholds. An unintended object on which both agree (SAM3 score $\geq 0.5$, OWLv2 score $\geq 0.2$, box IoU $\geq 0.3$) becomes an ignore region, on which predictions are not counted as false positives, if the scene is typical or the object's NPMI with every intended class is at most $0$; otherwise the scene is dropped, so that no unlabeled typical partner enters an atypical scene. Scenes that miss an intended class are dropped, and the typical and atypical sub-tiers at each $k$ are cut to the same size. Of the rendered scenes, $44.6\%$ enter the benchmark.

\noindent\textbf{Construction notes.} Three properties of the scenes as built qualify the design above. (i)~The atypical sub-tiers are not limited to low-NPMI pairs: pairs that never co-occur in COCO~\texttt{train2017} score above every co-occurring pair, so $39$ to $64\%$ of the anchor-partner pairs in the atypical sub-tiers have $N_{ij}{=}0$ (Tab.~\ref{tab:strange_tiers}); the NPMI medians cover the other pairs. \texttt{tier\_never} differs in asking for $N_{ij}{=}0$ for every pair, partner pairs included, with one exception: for $76$ of its $466$ scenes ($16.3\%$) the anchor is one of $11$ classes that co-occur with almost every other class (\eg, \emph{person}, \emph{car}, \emph{cup}), and the sampler falls back to the anchor's rarest partners (median $14$ co-occurring images, NPMI $-0.24$); $40$ of these scenes contain no pair with $N_{ij}{=}0$. Conversely, when no candidate meets the typical rule the sampler takes the highest-scoring one, so $3.5$, $10.3$, $16.5$ and $20.6\%$ of the typical anchor-partner pairs at $k = 1$ to $4$ have NPMI below $0.1$, most of them below $0$; the medians of Tab.~\ref{tab:strange_tiers} include them. (ii)~The caption request for the atypical sub-tiers $T_2$ to $T_6$ used the wording of the typical ones (Tab.~\ref{tab:tier_phrases}), so the two arms differ in the class set given to the caption model, not in the context wording; the final captions mention an unusual context in $6$ to $16\%$ of the atypical scenes and in none of the typical ones. (iii)~The benchmark boxes are the bounding boxes of the SAM3 masks; no audit compares them with the planner layout.

\subsection{User Study}
\label{app:strange:user-study}

We ran three blinded studies on randomly drawn scenes, with the sub-tier hidden and the order shuffled (interfaces in Fig.~\ref{fig:user_study_ui}). The raters were unpaid volunteers, and no personal data was collected. They used an initial version of STRANGE-Bench ($4{,}003$ scenes) that shares the layout, rendering and segmentation stages of App.~\ref{app:strange:pipeline} but was filtered by the Grounding~DINO audit of Stage~4 alone: one SD~3.5L render per scene, no second audit, no missing-class rule, and no balancing of the typical and atypical sub-tiers. That audit kept about $76\%$ of the renders. The released benchmark renders up to three times per scene and adds Stage~5, after which $44.6\%$ of the rendered scenes remain.

\noindent\textbf{Quality audit.} Raters judged $330$ scenes ($30$ per sub-tier) on four binary criteria: the image is photorealistic, the anchor is identifiable, the boxes are correct, and no unlabeled object is present. Of four raters, one passed every scene on three of the four criteria and is excluded; agreement among the other three is high (Gwet's AC1 $0.86$ to $0.99$ per criterion). Typical and atypical scenes at the same $k$ are rendered equally well: their pass rates differ by at most $7.8$ points (unlabeled objects), and anchor identifiability by at most $1.1$. \noindent\textbf{Pair plausibility.} Five raters saw $200$ class pairs as two names without an image ($40$ pairs that never co-occur in COCO and $40$ per NPMI quartile) and rated how often both occur in the same real-world scene on a five-point scale.
