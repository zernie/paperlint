\subsection{Selecting the AAS Atypicality Temperature \texorpdfstring{$\beta$}{β}}
\label{app:beta_sweep}

We select the AAS atypicality temperature $\beta$ once via a small ablation and hold the selected value fixed across all model families and full-COCO runs. Tab.~\ref{tab:beta_sweep} reports the sweep on Co-DINO \mbox{R-50} (AP), trained on the $25\%$ subset of COCO \texttt{train2017} (mini-COCO, $29{,}571$ images), and on DINOv3 ViT-B/16 fine-tuned on full COCO (mAP). 
$\beta = 3.5$ achieves the best or second-best score on every metric for both models, simultaneously balancing overall accuracy, in-domain typicality gap, and STRANGE-Bench coverage, and we adopt it for all main-paper experiments. 

\noindent\textbf{Tradeoff intuition.}
The sampling distribution $P(j \mid a) \propto \pi(j) \exp\big(\beta A(a, j)\big)$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:aas} has two endpoints. At $\beta = 0$, AAS samples paste classes uniformly and independently of the anchor, i.e.\ the XPaste baseline, and provides no debiasing signal; as $\beta \to \infty$, AAS concentrates almost all paste mass on the most atypical partners, which over-distorts the training-time joint and starts to hurt typical-pair coverage and overall accuracy. The sweep traces this tradeoff: $\beta = 2$ leaves residual gap ($\Delta^{\text{T-A}} = 8.1$ for Co-DINO, $9.5$ for DINOv3 FT, both worse than $\beta = 3.5$); $\beta = 5$ matches or beats $\beta = 3.5$ on a few metrics (Co-DINO typical AP, DINOv3 $\Delta_{1\text{-}5}$ on STRANGE-Bench) but drops on others (Co-DINO val AP, DINOv3 val mAP and gap), reflecting the over-tilting regime. $\beta = 3.5$ sits near the sweet spot for both families.
