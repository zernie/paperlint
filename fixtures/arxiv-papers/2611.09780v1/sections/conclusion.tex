\section{Conclusion}
Leaderboards not only measure model capabilities, but can also shape how developers allocate resources. 
Our model shows that this competition can lead to monoculture or specialization depending on the underlying production functions.
Similar production functions can induce monoculture, while comparative advantage can induce differentiated capability profiles and, in some settings, specialization.
We additionally characterize conditions under which diminishing returns and catch-up lead to convergence in performance over time.
In practice, these forces coexist: developers improve more where they lag while retaining persistent category-specific differences, alongside an increasingly crowded model frontier.
Our findings highlight that convergence in competitive performance need not imply monoculture in capabilities, leaving room for a future with many similarly competitive but differentiated models.
