A language model that keeps learning from the news sees the same claim many times, but many reports of a claim are copies of one source: wire stories, content shared within a media group, syndicated features and press releases. We ask whether LLM judges and continual learners treat such copies as independent confirmations. In a sample of 200,000 English news articles from CC-News, a MinHash detector links 18.9\% to a near-copy from another outlet; a text-similarity detector finds them with precision 0.97 once boilerplate is removed, links 92\% of edited reprints to their originals, but none of the rewrites of the same articles generated by phi4, Llama 3.2 or Gemini 2.5 Flash. Shown three identical reprints of a claim about an unknown person instead of one report, Llama 3.2 3B and phi4 14B raised the share of lies they accepted from 0.06 to 0.71 and from 0.21 to 0.90, and ignored a note that the reports came from one source; Gemini 2.5 Flash already believed a single report (0.93), and Gemini 2.5 Pro distinguished identical from differently worded reports and followed both the note and a provenance table. In a simulated continual-learning stream (detector errors drawn at their measured rates) with three attacks and three seeds, showing the judge one report per detected chain cut the copied lies it accepted by 0.22-0.24 for the two local judges, 0.16 for Gemini 2.5 Pro and 0.10 for Gemini Flash, at a mean cost of no more than 4.2 percentage points of accepted truths across the tested judges (largest upper 95\% confidence bound: 5.9 points). Text similarity misses reworded copies. A second test measures what share of a report's named entities are absent from an earlier look-alike: against 100 pairs of independent reports of the same event, labelled by two of the authors, it separates phi4 rewrites with AUC 0.98 (rewrites by Llama 3.2 and Gemini Flash: 0.98 and 0.99) and wrongly flags 1 pair; entity overlap alone does as well, TF-IDF and a current embedding model (Gemini) are statistically indistinguishable from it on this set (0.98 and 0.97), and other embeddings and cross-encoders score 0.66-0.94; quotations add little. With the detector-miss patterns observed on generated networks of ten rewrites, it cut the reworded-copy lies phi4 accepted from 0.67 to 0.20, or to 0.40 when the rewriter adds invented details. Neither report-counting nor chain-counting alone is a reliable defence across the tested attacks. Removing detected copies before judging makes the local judges less susceptible to copy attacks; the effect of provenance information depends on the judge; the entity-novelty test catches most of the tested rewrites but few natural same-source derivatives; and colluding sources remain the open problem.
