\subsection{Training Details}
\label{appendix:training}
We train all the evaluated models for two epochs on 1,000 randomly selected sample from PKU-SafeRLHF~\cite{ji2024pku} dataset with a batch size of two. Following~\cite{zou2024circuit}, we observed that adding a small number of samples for retaining models' general capability is necessary. Therefore we follow Circuit Breaker and additionally use 850 samples from Alpaca~\cite{alpaca} and 150 benign samples from XSTest~\cite{rottger2024xstest} during training Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Llama-3.2-3B to retain the models' capability during fine-tuning. To create preference pairs for the retain part of the dataset, we use a set of fixed refusal answers for the dispreferred choice and each model's benign response for the preferred choice. Preference pairs for PKU-SafeRLHF are used directly from the dataset itself.

Regarding ABS-Align's pruning strategy, we set pruning steps $t_p = 200$ and the pruning rate to 20\% such that every 200 steps safety neurons corresponding to one pruning iteration detected by TwinBreak are pruned. We use a linear cosine scheduler and AdamW as the optimizer for both models.


For finetuning the Gemma-2-2B-IT model, we use a learning rate of $1e-5$ and we set the beta for DPO to 0.1. We set weight decay to 0 and warmup ratio to 0.1. For Finetuning Llama-3-8B-Instruct, we use a learning rate of 2e-5, beta of 0.1, and warmup ratio of 0.1. For Llama-3.2-3B we use a learning rate of 2e-4 and beta of 0.3 with a warmup ratio of 0.1. Finally, for Qwen-3-4B we use a learning rate of 2e-4, a beta of 0.1, and a warmup ratio of 0.05.

\subsection{SORRY-Bench Black-box-style Adversarial Prompts}
\label{appendix:sorry_bench}
SORRY-Bench~\cite{xie2025sorrybench} has black-box twenty attack types in four categories namely writing style attacks, multi-languages, encoding and decryption, and presuasion techniques. We exclude multi-language and encoding and decryption attacks and select the ten categories in Table~\ref{tab:sorrybench_attacks} in our evaluations.


\begin{table}[t]
    \centering
    \small
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{0.9}
    \caption{SORRY-Bench black-box attack transformations used in our evaluation.}
    \label{tab:sorrybench_attacks}
    \begin{tabular}{p{0.28\linewidth} p{0.64\linewidth}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Attack Type} & \textbf{Description} \\
        \midrule
        Question & Harmful instruction rephrased as question \\

        Slang & Rewrites the harmful prompt using colloquial language and slang. \\

        Uncommon Dialects & Expresses the harmful request using less common dialects. \\

        Technical Terms & Rephrases the request using technical terminology. \\

        Role Play & Frames the harmful request within an assigned role or hypothetical persona. \\

        Misspellings & Introduces spelling or grammatical errors into the request. \\

        Authority Endorsement & Cites authoritative sources or institutions to support the request \\

        Misrepresentation & Misrepresents the harmful purpose of the request as benign. \\

        Evidence-based Persuasion & Uses facts to justify fulfilling the request. \\

        Expert Endorsement & Invokes the opinion of an expert. \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}
